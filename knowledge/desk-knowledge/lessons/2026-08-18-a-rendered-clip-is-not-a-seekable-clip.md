# A clip that plays perfectly is not necessarily a clip the compositor can SEEK — normalise it

**Purpose:** so the next "one black frame in the middle of the video" is diagnosed in a minute
instead of an evening, and so nobody re-runs the four experiments that ruled nothing out.
**When to read:** whenever a composited render shows a single black/missing frame, whenever Remotion
reports `No frame found at position …`, and before changing how HyperFrames clips are encoded.
**Do not duplicate:** if writing about clip encoding or the combo engine's hand-off, extend this file.

## Finding

The 022 v2 cut had five single black frames in 11,262 — the video layer gone for exactly one frame
while the burned-in caption above it still drew. The owner spotted four of them ("na 22 i 27 i 37
sekundi ima milisekundni blic… na 31.5 već malo ozbiljniji"). The same defect in the portrait render
was not subtle at all: it **hard-failed** the job with

    Compositor error: No frame found at position 83968 for source …/hf/s1.0.mp4

That error is the whole diagnosis, and it only ever appears in the log of the render that *fails* —
in 16:9 the compositor swallows it and returns an empty frame, so the log is clean and the defect is
visible only on the output.

**What it is NOT** (each of these cost a render, so do not repeat them):

| Suspected | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| The clip itself has a black frame | full sequential decode of every `hf/*.mp4` | no black frame anywhere |
| A timestamp gap or duplicate | PTS ladder of every clip | perfectly regular, exact frame counts |
| Keyframe seeking | re-encode the clip **all-intra** (`-g 1`) | dropouts stay at the SAME frame indices |
| A render-worker race | `--concurrency=1`, then a five-frame render | reproduces exactly, deterministic per frame |

What it actually is: the compositor seeks the mp4 directly, and the HyperFrames output is not shaped
the way it expects — the failure is in the CONTAINER, not the pixels, and not in anything the scene
author did.

## What to do

**Both steps are required. Normalising is necessary and NOT sufficient — measured, not assumed:**
after normalising every clip, the portrait render stopped failing entirely and the long cut went from
five dropouts to **one** (only the `t3d-hook` one healed; the four inside `t3d-window` survived
unchanged, at the same frame indices). Anyone who ships on the normalisation alone will ship four
black frames.

- **1 · Normalise every clip after it renders**, before it is ever composited: re-encode CFR at the
  exact fps and **clone a short tail past the end**, so the compositor is never asked for a frame at
  or beyond the last one. `buildNormaliseCommand()` in `pipeline/04-render/compile-hyperframes.mjs`,
  automatic for every HF clip. It never fails a render — a warning is enough, because an
  un-normalised clip still composites, it just risks the dropout *and* the hard failure.
- **2 · Repair the OUTPUT, every time.** `node scripts/repair-dropouts.mjs <video.mp4>` scans a
  rendered file for single-frame dropouts (near-black AND far darker than both neighbours) and
  repairs each by dropping the frame and letting `fps` repeat the previous one — same length, same
  timing, audio copied. It re-scans afterwards and exits non-zero if anything survived, so a clean
  exit is evidence. It copies nothing when there is nothing to fix, so run it unconditionally.
- **Do not reach for the pixels first.** A defect that is deterministic per frame, survives an
  all-intra re-encode AND survives `--concurrency=1` is not an encoding or a scheduling problem. Go
  and find the render that FAILS on it — the portrait cut named the cause in one line.

## Evidence

- 022 v2, 2026-08-17/18: dropouts at 4.50s, 22.40s, 27.00s, 31.60s, 37.33s of the long cut, all
  inside Three.js clips; the Short's render aborted at frame 203 on `cws-forgot`'s clip.
- After normalising: the Short renders clean end to end (0 compositor errors, 0 dropouts across
  1,664 frames) and the long cut keeps 4 of its 5. After the repair pass: 11,262 frames scanned,
  0 dropouts, duration and audio unchanged (6:15.45, aac 48 kHz stereo copied).
- `pipeline/04-render/compile-hyperframes.test.mjs` covers the normalise command;
  `scripts/repair-dropouts.test.mjs` covers the detector (including the two false positives it must
  NOT flag: a deliberate cut to black, and a hard cut between two bright scenes).
