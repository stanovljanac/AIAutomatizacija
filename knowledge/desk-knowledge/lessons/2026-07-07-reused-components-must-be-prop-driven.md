# Reused scene components must be prop-driven — a matching default masks a hardcode

**Purpose:** this file exists to record how a "reusable" Remotion component silently
rendered a previous video's text, and the check that prevents it.
**When to read:** read this when reusing any existing scene component in a new video's
scene-plan, or when authoring a component meant to be reused.
**Do not duplicate:** component craft rules live in
[MOTION_SPEC](../../../style/MOTION_SPEC.md); this is the reuse-specific trap.

## Finding

A component can *look* parametrized (props declared, defaults present) while actually
**hardcoding** its on-screen strings. The trap: when the first video's prop value equals
the hardcoded string, the bug is invisible — it only surfaces when the next video reuses
the component and the old video's words render on screen.

## Evidence

- **2026-07-07 — video 011, render v1**: S7 reused 009's `RecapCta`. The rendered frame
  showed 009's "stop chasing the money printer" (hardcoded line) and "Plug the boring
  leak" (a `title` prop that was computed and then **ignored** — line 66 rendered a
  literal). 009 never exposed it because its scene-plan title matched the hardcode.
  Caught by the perceptual still-review; fixed by making the strike line + title genuinely
  prop-driven ([PROGRESS 2026-07-07](../../../docs/PROGRESS.md)).

## Decision

Before reusing a component: **grep its JSX for literal strings** and verify every
on-screen word traces to a prop or a default that the new scene-plan overrides; when
authoring, never render a literal where a prop exists (the declared-but-ignored prop is
the exact failure shape). The 011 perceptual QA step (render stills of every scene, read
the words) is the safety net that caught it — keep reading the words on the stills.
Single data point → `draft`.
