# Angle variety is not motion design — hold the camera square and move the OBJECT

**Purpose:** this file exists so a 3D video is built around an object whose parts move, shot face-on,
instead of around text panels that a camera circles.
**When to read:** read before choosing the visual system of any video that uses Three.js, and before
adding an oblique or orbiting shot to any scene.
**Do not duplicate:** the numbers and the kit live in `style/MOTION_SPEC.md` §18 and D-069; extend this
file for any further finding about camera angle or object-level motion.


## Finding

Four Desk Lessons videos in a row (021–024) got more and more 3D, and the owner's verdict on the family
was that the dimension was being spent on the wrong thing: *"ne želim toliko uglova kamera od 45/65/75
stepeni u odnosu na objekat… drži se 90 osim kad baš želimo 3D dubinu i prikažemo neku ključnu ideju
koja ne može da stane u samu scenu"*, and, comparing with what other models produce, *"rastavljaju se
svakakvi delovi automobila, vraća, izvlači — to sve izgleda par excellence, a mi pravimo 3D chatove
teksta sa LLM modelom."* The subject on screen was still text; depth and a moving camera were standing
in for motion design. What reads as craft is an **object**: parts that travel, seat, mesh and come apart.

Two things turned out to be technical preconditions for that, not taste:

- **A face-on metal object is lit by what is behind the camera.** A flat plate square to the lens is a
  mirror of the wall behind the lens; in a dark room that is nothing, and the first render came back as
  dead-black gears. A bright uniform wall fixes visibility and makes everything read as a plastic toy.
  What worked: a small diffuse share on every metal (so cast shadows exist — a pure env-lit mirror shows
  none), a warm **spot** key whose pool falls off across the object, and a cool **raking** light almost
  in the object's own plane that lights every bevel and tooth. Depth comes from layered shadows.
- **When the camera is still, motion blur has to come from the parts.** `hf-world` smears along the
  camera path, which is zero here. `hf-mech` averages N sub-frames across the shutter instead, so a
  card sliding into a tray or an envelope leaving the chute blurs correctly.

## Evidence

Owner brief for 025, 2026-10-10 (the quotes above), which also supersedes the "~50/50 3D" target he set
on 2026-08-23. Look development on `templates/hyperframes/scenes/mv-look`: five lighting passes, frames
in `tmp/025/` — bright-toy (uniform env), near-black (gradient-only env), then spot + rake with
metalness 0.55–0.92. Measured on the 025 scenes: 8.5 fps at 1080p with 6 sub-frames (a 14.5 s scene in
51 s), all eleven clips in 15 minutes.

## Rule

- **Pick the object before the script is storyboarded**, and make it something with parts (025: a
  clockwork job engine). Every scene is that object doing something; text lives on the object's own
  plates, and the flat HUD only carries what must be read on a phone.
- **Camera square to the object by default.** Dolly and truck freely; yaw and pitch stay at 0. One
  oblique shot per video at most, and only for an idea that is about depth (025 s5: the machine opened
  along the lens axis to show there is nothing between the agent and the chute). A reveal may START
  off-square if it resolves square (025 s1's assembly).
- **Motion is the object's**: assembly and disassembly (`Part.place` runs both directions), a visible
  run of the mechanism, one part arriving and changing what the mechanism does.
- Light a face-on object with a falloff key and a raking light; never rely on environment light alone.
