# A well-written script cannot save a weak story — score story strength at the idea

**Purpose:** this file exists to explain why story strength (transformation, stranger
test, transferable framework, conflict) is scored at the idea-pass, before any script
exists, and what the five story tests are.
**When to read:** read this when picking/scoring a topic, framing an angle, or when a
draft "passes every craft rule" but still feels like it doesn't justify a video.
**Do not duplicate:** if writing about story/idea strength or the transformation test,
extend this file instead of creating a new one.

## Finding

A script can score high on every execution axis (visuals, pacing, hook, structure) and
still fail as a video because the **story is too small**. Execution review cannot catch
this — by then the weak story is already written. The failure signatures:

1. **No transformation** — the viewer's head ends where it started (hook line and
   closing line could swap places: 013 opened "fail loud", closed "fail loud").
2. **Feature showcase** — "my system has retries / halt / notifications" gives a
   stranger no reason to care; the story must be about THEIR risk and THEIR workflow.
3. **Symptom told as the story** — the incident (a 503) is only the symptom; the video
   is the philosophy behind it ("the most dangerous automation isn't the one that
   fails — it's the one that keeps going").
4. **Non-transferable lesson** — "retry on error" only serves our studio; a framework
   like "Draft → Check → Gate" transfers to the viewer's invoices, email, agents, code.
5. **No conflict / "wait, what?"** — every beat goes exactly as expected; the strong
   version builds a contrast (pipeline A shrugs-and-ships vs pipeline B halts-and-tells).

## Evidence

- **2026-07-12 — 013 "fail-loud" draft**: second-model review (owner: "agree with 95%")
  scored the finished draft visuals 10/10, pacing 9/10, hook 8.5/10 — but lesson 5.5/10
  and viewer usefulness 5/10: "a well-implemented implementation practice, not a big
  idea; would work better as one scene in a longer video about reliable AI workflows."
  Every problem above was present, and no craft rule caught any of them.
- Contrast cases that DID transform: 011 read-my-inbox ("everyone builds apps" → "point
  it at the inbox") and 012 studio-reveal ("Draft → Check → Gate" — a transferable
  framework).

## Decision

Story strength is scored **at the idea-pass, not discovered at script review**:

- A `transformation` scored category (weight 0.20) was added to the idea-stage rubric in
  [rubric.mjs](../../../pipeline/shared/review/rubric.mjs), and the `takeaway_present`
  hard gate now carries the stranger test (a feature list of our own pipeline is not a
  takeaway). Schema + config weights updated to match.
- The script-writing skill gained **Step 0.5 — the STORY TEST** (the five questions
  above, [SKILL.md](../../../.claude/skills/script-writing/SKILL.md)): if the brief's
  angle fails, push back to the idea/angle instead of writing.
- Sequencing corollary (same review): don't run two self-referential "our studio"
  videos back to back — alternate with practical viewer-facing videos.

**Round-2 craft corollaries (owner line-edits, 2026-07-12; now canon in STYLE_GUIDE §5/§8/§9).**
After the reframe fixed 80–90% of the problem, four transferable writing rules remained:
open on the idea not the anecdote (the reversal is the hook; avoid predictable "my thing
broke → best thing ever" setups); speak the viewer's language not the engineer's ("silent
vs loud", never "Pipeline A/B"); put the symbol on screen and keep it out of the voice (503
on-screen, "the model went down" spoken); and front the concrete symbol in the title/thumbnail
("Never Trust the Green Checkmark" > the abstract principle). Captured in the
`hook-and-voice-craft` auto-memory.
