# Constant background motion buries the point — the background moves only now and then

**Purpose:** this file exists so a scene's background is still by default and wakes only for a beat,
because motion that never stops stops meaning anything.
**When to read:** read before authoring any scene's ambient life (dust, tile shimmer, idle bobbing,
light drift, `FX.ambient`, `HW.motion` windows) and before deciding a video's tempo.
**Do not duplicate:** the numbers and the knobs live in `style/MOTION_SPEC.md` §17 and D-068; extend
this file for any further finding about background motion or overall tempo.


## Finding

The 024 Short was already 10% slower and measured 26% calmer than the long cut, and the owner still
judged it *"not good enough"*: the number tiles shimmered and the dust drifted under every second of
the video, and *"constantly through the whole video it loses the point"* — even though the same
shimmer is *"fine in some situations"*. Background life that never stops is not read as life; it is
read as noise competing with the line being spoken. A one-off device can be the opposite problem: the
gold training sweep was simply too fast (~0.58s across the block per ring), and a 5% global tempo
moves that by ~0.03s, which nobody sees.

## Evidence

Owner review of `content/024-frozen/short/video/final.mp4`, 2026-10-09 (screenshot: the `fzs-learn`
monolith mid-sweep under the "real training · 3 steps · one example" tag). Every `fzs-*` scene drove
tile twinkle, dust and idle spin from one always-running ambient clock (`M.amb(t)`), scaled to 0.9 but
never stopped. Sweep: `uShock` radius grows at `SPEED = 30` units/s from the top of a 44-row block
(row pitch 0.36 → ~16 units tall, ~17 units to the far corner) — ~0.58s per ring, three rings 0.32s apart.

## Rule

- The background is STILL by default (`motion.budget.ambient: []`); a scene opens a window
  (`props.motion.ambient: [[t0, t1]]`) only for a beat that the background life serves — an arrival, a
  reveal, a change of mood — and it runs at 0.81 there. A window marks a beat, not a scene.
- Keep the story moving instead: the subject, the camera on it, the take. §1's "something moves forward
  every ~3s" is carried by meaning, not by drift.
- A global tempo tunes the whole film; it does not fix one device. When the owner calls a single motion
  too fast, re-time it in its own scene and say so — don't promise the global percentage will cover it.
