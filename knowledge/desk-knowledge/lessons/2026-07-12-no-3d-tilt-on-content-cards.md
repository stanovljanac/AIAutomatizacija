# Content cards are never 3D-tilted — flat resting pose; 3D only as a transient reveal

**Purpose:** this file exists to record why persistent 3D tilts (rotateX/rotateY under
perspective) on cards/panels/terminals are banned and which reveal patterns are approved.
**When to read:** read this when authoring any HF scene with a card, panel, terminal, or
phone mock — especially before adding "cinematic" camera/dutch angles.
**Do not duplicate:** the canonical rule lives in
[MOTION_SPEC §5 "Card & panel angles"](../../../style/MOTION_SPEC.md); this file is the
evidence trail.

## Finding

A persistent 3D tilt on a content card reads as a mistake on a phone screen — the skewed
rectangle looks broken rather than cinematic, and its raised corner drifts over
neighboring text as the camera moves. Cards must rest **flat and face-on**; 3D rotation
is legitimate only as a **transient reveal**, never a resting pose.

## Evidence

- **2026-07-12 — 013 s3 `incident-503` rejected by the owner**: the dutch-angle terminal
  (rotationY −12°, rotationX 3°, rotationZ −4° under 1400px perspective) was "at a weird
  angle, going up over the text" — the tilted top corner crossed into the "503" headline.
  Fix that passed: rotations removed entirely, card moved below the headline.
- Same review approved two 3D **reveals**: the 013 demo-day flip card (Y-flip 180° to
  the other face) and requested more **stacked-deck reveals** (top card slides away to
  expose the card beneath).

## Decision

Canonical rule added to [MOTION_SPEC §5 "Card & panel angles"](../../../style/MOTION_SPEC.md)
(owner rule, 2026-07-12): flat resting pose; flip-reveal and stacked-deck reveal
approved as transient moves; small flat Z-rotation (≤3°) fine; environmental planes
(grid floors) exempt. Reserved-space rule (§0) applies to a card's travel path.
