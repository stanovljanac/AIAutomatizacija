# No invented anecdotes — script consequences as general truths, not claimed events

**Purpose:** this file exists to draw the line between allowed synthetic DEMO data and a
forbidden invented real-world OUTCOME in a script.
**When to read:** read this when a script beat is about to assert a specific personal
story or a concrete consequence ("then the client left", "we lost the deal") that did not
actually happen.
**Do not duplicate:** if writing about honesty/credibility in scripts, extend this file;
the workflow-realism angle lives in
[research the real workflow first](2026-06-14-research-real-workflow-first.md).

## Finding

Scripting a **specific claimed outcome we never actually experienced** (a named result, a
lost deal, a customer quote) erodes trust on a credibility-built channel — even when it's
"just" illustrative. Synthetic **demo data** is fine and must be labeled aloud; an invented
**real-world consequence** presented as something that happened is not. State consequences
as **general, illustrative truths** instead.

## Evidence

- **2026-07-07 — video 011 Short, 2nd-model review (owner-relayed)**: S5 read "left unread
  three days, the deal goes cold — sorry, we went with someone else." The reviewer: "ovo mi
  je jedino što zvuči malo izmišljeno… ceo kanal gradiš na poverenju." Replaced with a
  general truth — "the email that matters quietly becomes the email you forgot, buried under
  everything else" ([PROGRESS 2026-07-07](../../../docs/PROGRESS.md)).
- Contrast (allowed): the same script's S4 says "fake mail, real workflow" out loud — the
  inbox contents are **synthetic demo data**, disclosed, not a claimed event.

## Decision

Two separate rules, don't conflate them: **(a)** synthetic demo data is encouraged and
disclosed ("fake data, real workflow"); **(b)** invented real-world *outcomes* stated as
fact are banned — write the consequence as a general/illustrative truth unless the event
genuinely happened to us. Candidate for a `script-review` check. Single data point →
`draft`.
