# A post effect must sample the same image the frame shows

**Purpose:** this file exists so the green/magenta "glitch tiles" that appeared in the first fully-3D
render (024) are recognised on sight and never debugged from scratch again.
**When to read:** read before adding or changing any full-screen post effect in
`templates/hyperframes/_lib/hf-world.js` (or any composite shader), and whenever a 3D render shows
colour fringing that looks like whole objects changed colour.
**Do not duplicate:** if writing about the 3D post chain (motion blur, depth of field, chromatic
aberration, bloom), extend this file.


## Finding

Chromatic aberration was implemented as "take the finished pixel, then replace its red and blue with
samples taken a few pixels away **from the sharp scene texture**". Whenever the finished pixel was not
the sharp texture — the camera was smearing (motion blur) or the pixel was defocused (depth of field) —
the green channel came from the blurred image and red/blue from the sharp one. Bright tiles split into
**green faces with magenta digits**, worst on distant, out-of-focus tiles and in shake-heavy shots.

## Evidence

024 s2, frame at 7.5s (the 100-corrections volley under impact shake). Four 12-frame debug renders of
the same instant with toggles (2026-10-08): CA on → green/magenta blocks; CA off → clean; bloom off
and mipmaps off changed nothing. The shake made it worse because a 61 rad/s kick produced a large
per-frame camera delta, so the analytic motion blur was active on almost every frame of the shot.

## Rule

- Any effect that re-samples the scene must sample **the same image** the pixel already shows. Here:
  apply CA only where the frame is sharp (no smear, inside the focal dead-zone), radially, ≤ 2.5px.
- Keep impact shake slow (≈34 rad/s) and small; a fast shake turns every frame into motion blur.
- Debug a post-chain artefact with **toggles on a 12-frame render of the exact instant**, not by eye on
  the full cut — it took four renders and ~2 minutes to isolate.
