# "Plain" is missing detail, not missing content

**Purpose:** this file exists so "the scenes look plain" is never again answered by making objects
bigger, and so the floors in the frame gate are traceable to the measurement that set them.
**When to read:** read before authoring any scene, and before changing any threshold in
`pipeline/05-qa/lib/ink-coverage.mjs`.
**Do not duplicate:** if writing about frame fullness, detail density or the frame gate's floors,
extend this file.


## Finding

When the owner says a video looks *"obviously AI — every scene is too simple, plain and generic"*,
the defect is **not** that the frames are empty. It is that the things drawn in them carry no
readable content: pages are blank white bars, cards are an empty rounded rect with a dot and a line,
a grid of eighteen models reads as a UI loading skeleton. Filling more of the frame with the same
featureless shapes does not fix it and can make it worse.

## Evidence

Measured 2026-08-22 over 24 evenly-spaced frames per video, at 960×540, after the owner rejected
022 with the same verdict he had given 021:

| | ink coverage (median) | fine detail (median) | detail p25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 012 — the benchmark the owner locked as the FLOOR | 15.6% | 4.15% | 2.45% |
| 022 — rejected | **17.5%** | 3.92% | 1.81% |
| 021 — rejected as "slideshow with words on a plain background" | 17.6% | **2.04%** | 1.45% |

The first hypothesis — *the rejected videos have emptier frames* — is **false**. 022 is marginally
FULLER than the benchmark. Ink coverage does not separate them at all.

Fine detail density does. It counts pixels that depart from their own 3×3 neighbourhood, so readable
text, texture and material score while flat fills and smooth gradients do not, however large they
are. On that measure 021 sits clearly apart, and 022's bottom quartile sits well under 012's.

Two frames from 022 the gate flags, both confirmed by eye:
- **s21** (detail 1.7%) — three featureless white strips on an otherwise empty desk.
- **s7** (detail 1.6%) — two blank rectangles and a large "4.5×". Perfectly legible, and exactly
  the "simple and generic" shape the owner means.

## Decision

1. **Author to detail, not to size.** A big object does not clear the bar; a *readable* object does.
   Every page gets real synthetic text, every card real content, every surface a material.
2. **The rule is enforced in code, not prose** — `pipeline/05-qa/frame-gate.mjs` measures each
   scene's settled, caption-free frame (the same frame-choice logic as `04b-thumbnails`) against
   calibrated floors in `pipeline/05-qa/lib/ink-coverage.mjs`: detail ≥ 2.4% for body scenes,
   with coverage ≥ 8% and box ≥ 18% as the empty-frame backstop. `cta`/`outro` get much lower
   floors so the signature monolith close is never bullied into clutter; `hook` gets higher ones.
   Written up as [MOTION_SPEC §12](../../../style/MOTION_SPEC.md).
3. **State its limits when reporting it.** The gate finds plain and empty frames reliably and ranks
   021 worst exactly as the owner did (50% of scenes fail vs 19% for 022), but it does not reproduce
   his overall preference — 012 fails 29% of its own scenes. It is an authoring forcing function and
   a floor detector, never a substitute for the owner's verdict at Gate ③.

## Two mechanical traps, each paid for once

- **The floors only mean anything at 960×540** (540×960 portrait). A flat shape's detail contribution
  is its *perimeter*, so shrinking the frame makes every blank fill start scoring as texture — a
  blank bar scored 8.7% "detail" at 96×60 and 2.4% at 480×270. `ANALYSIS_SIZE` is fixed for this
  reason and must not become a knob.
- **Span must be measured on trimmed ink.** Untrimmed, a source chip in one corner plus the subject
  in the middle bracket the frame between them and report an 81%-tall subject that actually covers a
  third of the height.
